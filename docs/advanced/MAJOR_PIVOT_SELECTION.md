# MSAS V5.4 — Closed-price Major rolling and structure checkpoints

**Sources:** `Cau_truc_nang_cao_-_Final.pptx`, slides 5–7, 11, 13–14; `Mau_hinh_xac_nhan_ao_chieu_-_Final (1).pptx`, slides 2–4 and 8. The source PPTs are user-provided but are not published in this public repository. **Scope:** replay and pattern classification; NOT an entry/exit signal.

## What the slides explicitly require (S)

1. Local SH/SL are produced from *three CLOSED candles*. A pivot is not knowable on its center candle and cannot become structural until its eligibility is confirmed.
2. Main/External and Internal directions are distinct. Major High/Low must be updated as validated structure changes; they are NOT static levels and not necessarily the most recent two local swings.
3. A main reversal MSS **S+** is confirmed on the **current** closed candle when `Close > active Major High` in Main DOWN, or `Close < active Major Low` in Main UP. Tolerance is exactly **0**; equality does not cross. The previous candle's Close is not an extra condition from the slides.
4. A wick-only excursion **C** leaves Main unchanged; CISD **B** is a delivery early-warning and does not change Main.
5. On an S+ reversal, retain the opposing Major point and mark the newly needed Major as **not confirmed**. Do not invent a new pivot on the break candle.

## Deterministic implementation (I, provisional where the slides are incomplete)

- Read the *active* Major before a candle is processed. Evaluate the current Close against the opposing boundary first. This prevents a newly assigned boundary whose previous Close was already outside from suppressing an S+.
- Once a same-direction Close extension has been observed **since the current pair became effective**, a new Major pair may be promoted only after eligible **structurally confirmed** SH/SL candidates are known. Do not require a second artificial crossing of the old Major if a Close has already established the extension.
- For Main DOWN, prefer a new **lower SL** and the latest eligible **lower SH** preceding that low. A wick higher than the protected Major High cannot replace it as a bearish Lower High.
- For Main UP, a new **higher SH** and an eligible **corrective SL** following it may replace the current bounds. This is required by the user's XAUUSD 48-bar annotated example. Other valid pairing candidates and multiple nested degrees remain an unresolved policy.
- Only append role changes at actual structural confirmation; record the earlier original pivot time separately. The chart may anchor the label at that earlier candle *after the role becomes known*, never in the preceding replay prefix.
- The original three-bar/Inside-Bar implementation in `static/engine.js` remains unchanged. The rules above live only in `static/advanced_engine.js`, with source/assumption trace in `docs/advanced/MSS_CISD_RULEBOOK.md`.

## V6 — USER-AMENDED independent Major roles (2026-11-19)

V6 removes opposite-pivot gating. MH and ML update independently at the first closed bar carrying sufficient selected-Swing evidence. A Close break is evaluated first against the Major active before that bar and emits its primary continuation or reversal immediately; no prior-Close crossing is required. The breakout candle is represented by a running provisional extreme, not an invented confirmed pivot. A selected pivot centered on the breakout candle itself is not used as the break's new extreme; a real selected pivot from the surrounding wave may be adopted only when its own recognition bar has closed.

For Main UP, a selected SH beyond the broken level becomes MH immediately. Until a selected SL after that MH exists, the previous ML remains active; the lowest eligible later SL becomes ML, then the extreme is locked until the next Close break. For Main DOWN, the selected SL beyond the broken level becomes ML immediately. Its corrective MH is the latest selected lower SH preceding that ML and after the previous ML; if none exists, retain MH. This deliberate asymmetry is provisional and reproduces the protected user examples.

On UP→DOWN S+, keep the highest of the old MH and selected SHs after it, set ML null, and track `provisionalLow`. DOWN→UP is mirrored: retain the lowest valid ML, set MH null, and track `provisionalHigh`. Historical roles remain in `roleChanges`; active IDs alone move.

- XAU40: SH-14 / SL-17 becomes active at index **18**, the right-bar close for SL-17 (V5.4: 19), and remains 4001.5 / 3991.1 at index 24 with no `major_updated` there.
- XAU48: SH-34 is recognized at 35; corrective SL-37 becomes ML at index **38** (V5.4: 40). Main remains UP with 3396.648 / 3373.545 at indices 40 and 47.

Open policy questions remain: competing equal candidates, nested degree boundaries, session arbitration, and whether the asymmetric DOWN pairing should become a general symmetric rule after more labeled examples.

### V6 round 2 — a Major role cannot be born already broken

At role assignment close `t`, an SH candidate is admissible as MH only when every Close in `(SH.index, t]` is `<= SH.price`; an SL candidate is admissible as ML only when every Close in `(SL.index, t]` is `>= SL.price`. Equality is admissible because structural breaks remain strict. The rule is checked independently for extreme and corrective sides, including initialization and S+ reversal retention.

The BTC40 regression demonstrates the reason: bar 28 closes at 73865.2, above both active `SH-16 = 73795.4` and historical `SH-22 = 73845.8`. The event is one continuation against SH-16. SH-22 cannot then become MH on that same close, so bar 29 cannot emit a second continuation against it; MH remains SH-16 with a running provisional high until valid `SH-29` is recognized at bar 30.

If the strongest or latest policy candidate is already violated, candidate search continues among policy-eligible pivots. If none is valid, no historical pivot is forced into the role. This validity filter is source-safety infrastructure around the still-provisional competing-candidate and asymmetric corrective policies; it does not certify those policies as market ground truth.
